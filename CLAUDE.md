# Tyll marketing website

Rebuild of the public marketing website for Tyll (tyll.ai).

## About Tyll

Tyll is a hospitality operations platform. It helps hotels bring information from their existing systems together, identify problems, coordinate departments and track results.

## Audience and tone

- For hotel owners, general managers and hospitality groups.
- Luxurious and minimal.

## Brand elements to preserve

- Existing logo
- Cinematic hotel reception opening
- Premium typography
- Hand-drawn checkmark animation
- Black / white / silver visual identity

The owner provides the official brand assets, website references and research document. Use those, not approximations, wherever they exist.

## Key facts (from the Step 1 audit, see AUDIT.md)

- Live site: tyll.ai runs on Railway (deployed by a friend with `railway up` from a local clone; needs `git pull` first). This computer has no Railway access.
- Demo repo: github.com/giulionovelli25-creator/Demo-Tyll (Next.js). Read-only for this project.
- Node is at `~/.local/share/node-lts/bin`, not on the default PATH. Prefix commands with `PATH="$HOME/.local/share/node-lts/bin:$PATH"`.
- The demo's permanent local copy is `~/tyll-demo` (runs on port 3210). Never edit it, and never run `npm init`/`npm install` with the shell's working directory inside it.
- Stack: Astro 7.3, static output. Four languages (EN, DE, AR, IT) with routes in `src/i18n/routes.ts` and copy in `src/i18n/en.ts`, `de.ts`, `ar.ts`, `it.ts`; solution text in `src/content/solutions.ts` uses `t(en, de, ar, it)`.
- Arabic is right-to-left: `<html dir>` comes from `dirOf()`. Use logical CSS (`margin-inline-start`, `inset-inline-end`, `text-align: start`). In component styles, RTL overrides must be written `:global([dir='rtl']) .x`, because Astro scopes a bare `[dir='rtl']` to the component and it never matches. Arabic font: Noto Sans Arabic in `public/fonts/`, Arabic characters only. Walkthrough and step frames stay left-to-right because the demo is English. The language switch shows العربية (not AR) using a tiny font subset.
- Arabic and Italian text, and all legal pages, are drafts awaiting native-speaker and legal review.
- Architecture (31 Oct 2026, product focus): homepage = minimal hero ("Know exactly what to buy. Every week.", Try the demo → demo.tyll.ai and Get your free report → #report) → Available now (Purchasing Prediction, Stock Count and Waste, Reconciliation, linking to the forecasting, stock and finance pages) → the hotel scroll story → the two sourced numbers → Works with the systems you already use (the logo band) → the free purchasing report form, centred, on the dark wave section (`ReportForm.astro`: one button opens it, then three steps unfold one at a time, finished steps fold to a check mark and a summary; POS systems are tap-to-choose. It composes an email to info@tyll.ai, with the address kept on screen for webmail users; there is no backend). Products are named the same everywhere: Purchasing Prediction, Stock Count and Waste, Reconciliation (URLs unchanged). Every demo button reads "Try the demo". What is on sale vs coming soon lives in `src/config/products.ts` (menu badges, overview badges, solution page banner; the homepage's What we're building next and About sections were removed at the owner's request, 31 Oct 2026). The only fictional-data footnote is in the footer: "Example screens use fictional data." Solutions menu with six solution pages built from `src/content/solutions.ts`; FAQ in the menu and footer.
- Demo screenshots live in `src/assets/demo/`. Captures that create records were taken from a throwaway copy of the demo, never the real one. Photo sources are in `src/assets/photos/CREDITS.md`.
- Per-solution demo deep links: `src/config/demo.ts`.
- Homepage scroll story (`src/components/home/HotelStory.astro`, 3 Nov 2026): four steps in the owner's chosen order (Reconciliation, Stock Count and Waste, Purchasing Prediction, the result). The hotel starts as a fine line drawing surrounded by a mess; each step shows its problem, then fixes it and completes one part of the building (centre, left wing, right wing), with the camera going in to the storeroom and the bar; the last step adds the sign, evening light and guests. Each fix reveals a close framing of a real demo capture (`public/media/story/`, avoid "House wine"). `b` from 0 to 4 comes from the text's position; each piece carries its own window, so scrolling back undoes everything. No animation library. Only guests, waiter and data pulses run on time, and only on screen. Reduced motion or no JavaScript: the finished hotel. The scene stays left to right in Arabic. No bottles in the restaurant or bar. The explorer on solution pages keeps the list-beside-real-screen pattern from 1320 px. Do not bring back invented examples or slogans.
- Calendly's own cookie notice stays on (no `hide_gdpr_banner`), because its booking page loads its own services (Stripe, Segment, reCAPTCHA, Google Analytics) inside the iframe. The legal pages' "On this page" list is built from the rendered `<h2 id>` headings in `LegalShell.astro`.
- Marketing photos and video must not feature alcohol (Gulf market). The site's deep colour is night blue (story section); amber is the warm accent (Coming soon, findings). Sources in `src/assets/photos/CREDITS.md`.
- Solution pages (17 Oct 2026, `SolutionPage.astro`): opening photo (72vh at most, so the next section shows in the first screen) → warm-ivory context in the centred column: the problem and how Tyll helps as three joined steps (point and fine line, action above its explanation) beside a second photo set into the page with rounded corners, about 58/42 with space between; the photo's side alternates and its shape and height follow a three-step cycle (`context--v0/1/2`); copy and photo rise in gently once (not with reduced motion) → one product explorer (`src/components/ProductExplorer.astro`, data in `src/content/explore.ts`) → a charcoal CTA panel with the Tyll silver wave (`ribbon()` from `src/scripts/wave.ts`, moving like the homepage wave only while on screen, still under reduced motion) on a light band, then the other solutions as photos. Explorer: each part is a complete block of the demo screen (focus rectangles are snapped to the gaps between cards — re-snap if a capture changes), with a ring on the detail; `fade: true` marks parts whose content carries on and offers the whole screen. Up to 900 px wide the part is shown at a readable scale (≥ 0.8) and scrolls sideways. The part sits on the product's own ground (`--mist`) with no blurred backdrop. From 901 px the explorer is sized to the screen height so the tabs, the screen and its explanation fit together (the script measures the tabs and the tallest explanation into `--ex-tabs`/`--ex-notes`); it never gets narrower than 900 px, where demo text would be too small, so on short screens such as 1366 × 768 the explanation sits just below the fold. Whole screen and an Enlarge viewer with every screen are always available. Every explanation must describe only what its screen shows.
- The closing section's silver wave is one coherent ribbon computed in `src/scripts/wave.ts` (a fixed check-like curve plus travelling swells; every line shares the shape, offset like threads). The component redraws it at about 30 fps only while on screen; a 16 s cycle loops seamlessly; reduced motion shows the first frame, which is also server-rendered.
- Homepage statistics (`WhyItMatters.astro`, 2 Nov 2026): "Two numbers worth knowing", two sourced figures on one white panel with no photo (the owner rejected a chef, a manager at a desk, a receptionist and a warm lobby). 10+ counts up as ten system names tumble into a heap (they straighten on hover); then, only after that, $7 counts up as seven coins land on a scale against one and tip it. An arrow leads from the first to the second. $7 for every $1 comes from the WRI and WRAP hotel study and concerns kitchen food waste only. Reduced motion or no JavaScript: the final frame.
- Hero (2 Nov 2026): the headline (Newsreader Light, two lines on desktop, each sentence on one line from 640 px up), one short line saying what Tyll covers, one solid button (Try the demo) and a quiet text link (Get your free report) over the film; the Tyll mark is in the navigation only.
- UX cues are deliberately few: demo buttons that all read "Try the demo" and call buttons that all read "Book a call", an All Solutions link at the end of each solution page, and one line on the booking page saying what the call is. No urgency, testimonials or invented claims.
- Every Explore Solutions button links to the solutions overview page; the header's Solutions dropdown is the only dropdown.
- Book a Call is a split screen (booking left, sunlit lobby photo right); once Calendly opens, the photo hides and the calendar takes the full width. Calendly still loads only after the click.
- Demo screens are full 1440×1240 captures (2× pixels). The explorer may frame a close-up, but the whole screen must always be one click away (Whole screen, Enlarge); never stretch or edit a capture.
- Legal pages (`LegalShell.astro`): a pearl title band with a short silver rule, and an "On this page" list built from the rendered `<h2 id>` headings, sticky beside the text from 1100 px with the current section marked. The booking page has a pearl ground with a faint still silver wave.
- External settings are `PUBLIC_*` variables (see `.env.example` and LAUNCH.md). Legal details live in `src/config/legal.ts` and must never be guessed.
- `npm run launch-check` lists launch blockers. `npm run test:e2e` runs the browser tests (needs Google Chrome and the dev server).

- Palette (2 Nov 2026): light first. Warm off-white page (`--page`), white surfaces, and the new demo's ink, line and accent tokens, all in `src/styles/global.css`; no hard-coded colours outside it (the one exception is the theme-color meta tag). Roles (`--text-1..3`, `--hair`, `--gold`, `--shadow-*`) flip under `.on-dark` and `[data-dark]`. On the homepage only the closing report section is a dark section, plus the hero film; the footer is light. Type (3 Nov 2026, after Aman's Lyon and Whitney and Rosewood's Austin, which are paid fonts): Newsreader Light for all headings, Source Sans 3 for everything else, Noto Naskh Arabic for Arabic headings, self-hosted with metric-matched fallbacks. Headings are light, open and never tightly spaced. Hero 56 to 72 px on desktop, one solid button and a quiet text link.
- Hero film (2 Nov 2026): one continuous 12 s loop with 0.5 s dissolves and no black frames (reception bell, dim kitchen, lobby chandelier), one grade, built by `docs/hero-film-master.sh`, see `docs/hero-video-brief.md`. Upright screens up to 820 px get their own 3:4 cut and poster; on upright phones the film is a window at the top with the words on the light page below. Keep bottles and signage out of frame.
- Integration logos (`src/components/home/Integrations.astro`, data in `src/config/integrations.ts`, files in `public/logos/integrations/` with `SOURCES.md`): the makers' real files only, never retyped or redrawn; shown in the makers' own colours; CSS marquee of 36 s, paused by hover, touch or focus; a still centred grid under reduced motion. Oracle and Lightspeed say their logos need a licence, so written permission is needed before this goes live.

- Solution page headlines all start with "Know" and say something concrete; avoid slogans. The Purchasing explorer leads with What to buy (on sale) and marks the rest Coming soon. Demo data contains "House wine" (fc-2, fc-5): the close-ups avoid it, but the whole-screen views still show it, so it must be removed in the demo and the screens recaptured.
- Order and emphasis (2 Nov 2026): Purchasing Prediction comes first everywhere (`solutionKeys`). The homepage's Available now keeps three equal cards side by side, as the owner asked. The overview shows what is on sale first and coming-soon products below, smaller and quieter; coming-soon pages lead with Book a call. `src/pages/404.astro` is the error page.

## Rules

- **Do not assume the existing website's source files are available here.** They are not in this folder unless the owner adds them.
- **Ask for missing assets or repository access** instead of guessing or inventing substitutes.
- **Never publish or deploy to the live website without the owner's explicit approval**, given in chat for that specific deploy.
